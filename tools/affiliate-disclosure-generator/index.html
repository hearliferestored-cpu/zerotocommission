How to use what you copied

  1. Put it with the recommendation. In the post, video or email itself, close to the link. A note in your bio or on an About page isn't enough on its own.
  2. Make it hard to miss. First line of the caption, early in the video, not hidden in a block of hashtags or only behind "more".
  3. In video, say it and show it. The FTC says people are more likely to notice a disclosure that is both spoken and on screen.
  4. Keep it true. Edit the wording so it matches your situation exactly. A disclosure doesn't make an untrue claim acceptable.

Platform labels (like a "paid partnership" toggle) are worth using as well, but the FTC says not to rely on them alone. For the reasons behind each line, and wording to avoid, see Affiliate Disclosure Examples.

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. It turns the FTC's published examples into ready-made wording. Rules differ by country and change over time, and each affiliate program and platform has its own terms. Check them, and talk to a lawyer if you need legal advice.

Do you save what I type?

No. The generator runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to us or stored.

Do I need a disclosure if I'm not paid?

If you bought something yourself and have no relationship with the brand, the FTC says there's nothing to disclose. The moment you use an affiliate link or get something for free, you do.
