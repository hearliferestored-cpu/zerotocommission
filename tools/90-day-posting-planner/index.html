How the plan is built

  • Weeks 1–2: set up. Profile, link, disclosure, one program and one product, and a list of ideas before you post.
  • Weeks 3–6: post on schedule. Mostly helpful posts, with a product mentioned only now and then.
  • Week 7: first review. Look at which posts people watched longest and clicked, and do more of those.
  • Weeks 8–11: double down. Repeat the formats that worked, test one new idea a week.
  • Weeks 12–13: decide. Keep going, change the product, or narrow the topic, based on your own numbers.

This is a plan for effort, not a forecast. Many people earn little or nothing in their first 90 days, and that's normal. What you'll have is 90 days of practice and real numbers to decide your next step.

Questions

How many times a week should a beginner post?

As many as you can keep up for three months. A steady 3 a week usually teaches you more than 14 in week one and then nothing.

Why so few product posts?

People follow accounts that help them. If every post is a pitch, there's little reason to watch. Helpful posts build the attention your occasional recommendation needs.

What should I track?

Views, average watch time, profile visits, link clicks and sales. Our commission calculator turns clicks and sales into a click-to-sale ratio.
