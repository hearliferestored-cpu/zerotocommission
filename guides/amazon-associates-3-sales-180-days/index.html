Amazon Associates' 3 Sales in 180 Days Rule, Explained

You can join Amazon Associates in minutes, but your account isn't really approved until you make three qualifying sales within 180 days. Here's what the rule says, what counts, what happens if you miss it, and how to give yourself a fair chance.

What the rule says

Amazon's help pages say that after you sign up, the Associates team reviews your application once you've driven qualified sales: at least three within the first 180 days. Personal orders don't qualify. Once you reach three, Amazon says it reviews your application within a day or two.

So signing up gives you links straight away, but the 180-day clock is a trial period. The review only happens after three sales.

What counts as a qualifying sale

  • A purchase made by someone else through your tagged Associates link, under the program's rules.
  • Not your own orders. Amazon says personal orders don't qualify. That includes asking friends or family to buy just to hit the number, which also breaks the rule against offering incentives for using your links.
  • Returned or cancelled orders may not count. Check your reports in Associates Central rather than counting clicks.

What happens if you don't make 3 sales in time

Amazon says your application can be withdrawn if there weren't enough qualifying sales within 180 days of submitting it. Amazon also says it can't reinstate an account or Associates ID after it's been rejected.

Amazon's help page also says you're welcome to reapply once your site is established. If you do, you get a new Associates ID, and you must replace the old links on your pages with tagged links from the new account.

What Amazon checks when it reviews you

Hitting three sales isn't automatic approval. Amazon reviews every site you listed in your application:

  • Websites: you must own the site, it needs robust original content (Amazon's rule of thumb is at least 10 posts), and content should be recent, generally within the last 60 days.
  • Social media pages: Amazon accepts Facebook pages (not personal profiles), Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, TikTok and Twitch. It says pages must be established, with a substantive number of organic followers, "in most cases, at least 500", and public.
  • Disclosure: you must identify yourself as an Amazon Associate with Amazon's required wording.

If an application is rejected, Amazon says it can't reassess it. That's why it pays to get your site or page in order before you start the clock.

How to give yourself a fair chance

  1. Build first, sign up second. The 180 days start when you apply. Publish your first 10 or so genuinely useful posts or videos before you sign up, so the clock isn't running while you have nothing live.
  2. List the right site. Put the exact URL of the page or channel you'll actually use in your application, and keep the list up to date.
  3. Answer buying questions. Content that helps people choose ("which one is better for…", "what to check before buying…") tends to lead to purchases more than general content does.
  4. Disclose properly. Use Amazon's sentence, "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases", plus a plain disclosure in each post. Our free disclosure generator builds both.
  5. Don't game it. No buying your own products, no asking friends to buy, no offering rewards for using your link. Those break the rules and can get the account closed.

Nobody can promise you'll reach three sales in 180 days. Many new accounts don't. If Amazon doesn't fit where you are right now, other programs don't have this rule; see affiliate programs with no follower minimum.

Questions beginners ask

Does the 180 days start when I sign up?

Amazon's help pages say you have 180 days after your application is submitted.

Can I buy through my own link to reach 3 sales?

No. Amazon says personal orders don't qualify.

Can I reapply after my application is closed?

Amazon's help pages say you're welcome to reapply once your site is established, but a rejected account or ID can't be reinstated. You'd get a new ID and need to update your links.

Do I need 500 followers to join?

You can sign up without them. For social media pages, Amazon says it usually expects at least 500 organic followers when it reviews your account.
