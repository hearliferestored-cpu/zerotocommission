Affiliate Disclosure Examples: FTC Wording for Every Platform

If you earn a commission when someone buys through your link, you need to say so, clearly, every time. Here is copy-paste wording for short videos, captions, YouTube, websites and email, plus the phrases that sound like a disclosure but aren't clear enough.

Do you need an affiliate disclosure?

Yes, if you get anything of value for a recommendation. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) calls a commission, a free product or any other payment a "material connection", and it expects you to disclose it so people can decide how much weight to give your recommendation. The FTC is clear that this is your responsibility as the person posting, not the brand's or the platform's.

Affiliate programs and social platforms usually require disclosure in their own terms too. If you post from outside the US, the FTC says US rules can still apply when your post is likely to reach US consumers, and your own country's rules may apply as well.

The four rules behind every good disclosure

  1. Put it with the recommendation. The disclosure belongs in the post or video itself, close to the link or the claim. A note on your profile, your "About" page or the bottom of your website is not enough on its own.
  2. Make it hard to miss. Don't hide it behind a "more" button, at the very end of a video, or in a pile of hashtags.
  3. Use plain words. "Ad", "sponsored" or a simple sentence like "I earn a commission if you buy through my link" works. Shorthand doesn't.
  4. In video, say it and show it. The FTC says the disclosure should be in the video, not only in the description, and that people are more likely to notice it when it's both spoken and on screen.

Affiliate disclosure examples you can copy

Adjust the wording so it's true for you. If you only may earn a commission, say "may". Only add "at no extra cost to you" if you've checked that it's true for that offer.

Short videos (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts)

Said out loud, early in the video"Quick note: if you buy through my link, I earn a commission."
On-screen text, held long enough to read#ad · I earn a commission if you buy through my link
First line of the caption, before "more"#ad I earn a commission if you buy through the link in my bio.Put it first. Many viewers never expand the caption.

Link in bio and bridge pages

Right above or next to the linkI earn a commission on purchases made through these links.This is in addition to the disclosure in each video, not instead of it.

YouTube videos and descriptions

In the video"The links in the description are affiliate links. If you buy through them, I earn a commission."
Top of the description, near the linksI earn a commission on purchases made through the links below.The FTC's FAQ says to disclose both in the video and near the links in the description.

Pinterest pins

Start of the pin description#ad I earn a commission if you buy through this pin's link.

Websites and blog posts

Before the first link in the postI get commissions for purchases made through links in this post.This is the example sentence the FTC itself uses for affiliate marketers.
Right next to an individual link(paid link)The FTC says "paid link" right next to an affiliate link should be adequate.

Email

Near the top, before the first linkThis email contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, I earn a commission.

Amazon Associates

Where Amazon's agreement requires itAs an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.Amazon requires this specific sentence. Check the current Associates Operating Agreement for where it has to appear, and still disclose in each post.

Promoting your own product

Anywhere you recommend something you sellThis is my own product, so I earn money if you buy it.It's the same principle. We use this wording for our own course.

Disclosures that aren't clear enough

These come up constantly, and the FTC's own guidance says they can fall short:

AvoidWhy
"affiliate link" on its ownThe FTC says people might not understand that it means you are paid for purchases. Add a plain sentence, or use "paid link".
#sp, #spon, #collabThe FTC calls these vague or confusing.
"thanks" or "ambassador" aloneDoesn't tell people you were paid.
"commissionable link", "buy now"The FTC says these are probably not clear disclosures.
Disclosure only in your bio or About pagePeople see individual posts without ever visiting your profile.
Buried in a block of hashtagsThe FTC specifically says not to mix it into a group of hashtags or links.
Only at the end of a video, or only behind "more"Many people never get that far.
Only the platform's built-in labelThe FTC says not to assume a platform tool is enough on its own. Use it in addition to your own wording.

Affiliate disclaimer vs affiliate disclosure

People use both words. An affiliate disclaimer usually means a page on your website explaining that you earn commissions. That's a good thing to have, but the FTC says a single disclosure on your home page isn't sufficient, because people read individual posts and watch individual videos without seeing it. The disclosure in each post is the part that does the work.

Honesty matters beyond the disclosure

A disclosure doesn't make a misleading post acceptable. The FTC also says you can't talk about your experience with a product you haven't tried, and you can't make claims about a product that need proof the seller doesn't have. Don't invent results, testimonials or income screenshots. If you wouldn't be comfortable repeating a sales page's claims in your own words, that's a sign to pick a different offer. Questions beginners ask

Does "#ad" count as an affiliate disclosure?

The FTC lists "ad", "advertisement" and "sponsored" as clear terms when they're placed where people will see them. For affiliate links, a short sentence like "I earn a commission if you buy through my link" tells people exactly what the relationship is, so many creators use both.

Do I need to disclose if I'm not paid?

No. The FTC says that if you're just recommending something you bought yourself and have no relationship with the brand, there's nothing to disclose. The moment you use an affiliate link or receive something free, you do.

Is there an affiliate disclosure generator I can use?

You can use one, but check the result against the four rules above. Most generators produce a website disclaimer page, which is useful but doesn't replace a disclosure in each post or video.
