Affiliate Marketing Without a Website: Bridge Pages and Other Options

You don't need a website or a blog to start affiliate marketing. Here are the ways beginners share affiliate links without one, how a bridge page works, a layout you can copy, and the trade-offs to know about.

Can you do affiliate marketing without a website?

Yes. A website is one way to share affiliate links, not a requirement. Many beginners start with a short-form video account and a single link in their profile. What you do need is a place where people find your content, a link they can click, and a clear disclosure that you may earn a commission.

One thing to check first: some affiliate programs ask for a website or social media profile when you apply, and some restrict where or how you may post links. Read each program's terms before you start.

What is a bridge page in affiliate marketing?

A bridge page is a short page that sits between your profile link and the offer. Its job is to help the visitor understand what they're about to see, who it may suit, and that you earn a commission, so they arrive at the sales page informed instead of confused.

It isn't a website. You can build one with a link-in-bio or one-page site tool, many of which have free plans. It doesn't need a custom domain or any code.

Why bother with one?

  • Context. Someone who taps a link in a video often doesn't remember exactly what was said. A bridge page reminds them.
  • Honesty. It's a natural place to say who the offer is not for, and what you did and didn't check.
  • Disclosure. It gives your disclosure a clear, visible spot right under the button.

A bridge page layout you can copy

Top to bottom:

  1. Headline: "A beginner-friendly look at [topic]"
  2. Two-sentence introduction: who you are and what this page explains.
  3. Who it may suit / who it may not: two short lists.
  4. What you actually did to look into it: for example, "I read the full sales page, the refund policy and the affiliate terms." Only what's true.
  5. The button to the offer.
  6. Disclosure, directly under the button.
  7. Three short FAQs a visitor might have.
  8. A contact line and footer.
Disclosure under the buttonI earn a commission if you buy through this link. I only recommend things I've looked into, and I've told you above what I did and didn't check.

What not to do on a bridge page

  • Don't pretend to be the seller, or copy their branding so visitors think they're on the official site.
  • Don't add fake countdown timers, invented scarcity or made-up results.
  • Don't describe experiences with the product you haven't had.
  • Don't hide where the link goes. People should know they're heading to a sales page.
  • Don't break the affiliate program's rules about landing pages or how you describe the product.

The trade-offs of not having a website

Starting without a website is cheaper and simpler. The trade-off is that you're building on platforms you don't control: their rules, reach and link policies can change. That's one reason some creators add an email list over time, so they can reach people who asked to hear from them. It's not something you need on day one.

Questions beginners ask

Do I need my own domain name?

No. Free link-in-bio and one-page tools give you a working page without one. A domain is optional and can come later.

Is a bridge page required?

No. You can link straight to an offer. A bridge page is useful when your videos can't fit all the context a buyer needs, or when you want a clear spot for your disclosure.

What's the best link in bio for affiliate marketing?

Any tool that works on your platform and whose terms allow affiliate links. Check that first, and make sure you can add a visible disclosure next to your buttons.

Can I put affiliate links directly in my TikTok or Instagram bio?

Often yes, but each platform has its own rules about who can add a clickable bio link and what links are allowed. Check the platform's current rules and the affiliate program's terms.
