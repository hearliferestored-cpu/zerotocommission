Affiliate Marketing Without Social Media: 6 Honest Options

You can do affiliate marketing without TikTok, Instagram or Facebook. People still need a way to find you, though, so you're swapping social feeds for something else: search, email or communities. Here are six options, what each one takes, and the trade-offs.

Can you do affiliate marketing without social media?

Yes. Affiliate marketing only needs three things: people who trust you, a useful recommendation and a link. Social media is one way to reach people, not the only one. The main difference is speed: social platforms can show a new account's videos to strangers quickly, while most non-social options take longer to build up.

1. A blog or small website

Write helpful pages that answer specific questions in your niche, such as "best budget planner app for students", and link to products where they genuinely help. Search traffic can keep coming for months, but new sites often take a long time to show up in search results. Disclose affiliate links near the top of each page (examples here).

2. YouTube long-form videos

Some people don't count YouTube as social media, because most viewers arrive through search. Tutorials, reviews and "how to" videos can be made with screen recordings and a voiceover. Put the link in the description with a clear disclosure.

3. An email newsletter

Email is the one channel you own: if a platform changes its rules, your list stays yours. The catch is that a list needs a source of sign-ups, which is usually a website, a search-friendly page or a free download. See how to build an email list for affiliate marketing. Some programs, including Amazon's, don't allow their links in emails, so check before you send.

4. Q&A sites and forums

Answering questions where people already ask them can work, if the community allows it. Many forums and subreddits ban affiliate links or self-promotion, and breaking that rule gets accounts banned. Lead with a full, useful answer, link only where it's allowed, and say when a link is an affiliate link. Our link rules checker covers where links are allowed on several platforms.

5. Writing platforms like Medium

Platforms with built-in readers let you publish without building a website first. Rules about affiliate links differ by platform and change over time, so read the current policy and disclose each link.

6. Paid ads

You can skip social media by paying for search or display ads, but you're spending money before you know whether an offer converts. Many affiliate programs also restrict ads that bid on their brand name. For a beginner on no budget, we wouldn't start here.

Which option should a beginner choose?

  • You like writing and can be patient: a blog or small website, with an email list once people arrive.
  • You're comfortable with video, but not social feeds: YouTube long-form tutorials.
  • You already know a topic well: answering questions in communities that allow links, plus a simple page to send people to.
  • You want the fastest way to reach strangers with no budget: honestly, that's usually short-form video on social platforms. See faceless affiliate marketing if showing your face is what's putting you off.

Rules that still apply without social media

  • Disclose every affiliate link, close to the link, in plain words.
  • Follow each program's rules on where its links can go. Some ban email, some ban ads on their brand name.
  • Follow each site's rules on self-promotion and links.
  • Don't make income or results claims about the products you promote.

Questions beginners ask

Is affiliate marketing without social media harder?

Usually slower to start, not necessarily harder. Search and email can keep working for a long time once they're set up.

Can I do affiliate marketing without social media or a website?

It's possible through email, communities or writing platforms, but very limited. Most people need at least one place where others can find them. See affiliate marketing without a website for the reverse case.

Is Pinterest social media?

It's often described as a visual search engine, because many people use it to search for ideas. If you're happy with that, Pinterest pins can link straight to an offer or a page, within Pinterest's rules.

Do affiliate programs require social media accounts?

Not always. Many sign-up forms ask for "a website or social profile", and a blog, YouTube channel or newsletter usually counts. Some programs do set follower or traffic minimums. See programs with no follower minimum.
