How to Become an Affiliate Marketer: 8 Steps From Zero

You don't need a degree, a license or an audience to become an affiliate marketer. You need a topic, one program, a place to post and the patience to keep going while it starts slowly. Here's the whole path in eight steps.

What an affiliate marketer actually does

An affiliate marketer recommends other companies' products using a personal tracking link and earns a commission when someone buys through it. Day to day, that means four things: researching products worth recommending, making content that helps people (short videos, pins, posts or articles), telling people clearly that you earn a commission, and reading your numbers to see what works. You don't make the product, take payments or handle support.

Do you need qualifications, a license or a business?

  • No degree or certificate is required to join affiliate programs.
  • Disclosure is required. In the US, the FTC expects you to tell people clearly when you earn from a recommendation. See disclosure examples.
  • Commissions are taxable income. In the US you report them on your tax return, and some programs send a tax form once you pass their reporting threshold. Rules differ by country; a tax professional can tell you what applies to you.
  • Registering a business isn't needed to sign up for most programs, but local rules vary. Many beginners start as individuals.

How to become an affiliate marketer in 8 steps

1. Pick a topic you can stick with

Choose a specific problem you can talk about for months, such as "meal prep for night-shift workers" rather than "health". A narrow topic makes it easier to know what to post and which products fit.

2. Choose one place to post

Pick one platform and learn it, rather than spreading thin. If you don't want to be on camera, faceless short-form video is one option. If you have no followers yet, read where beginners' first viewers come from.

3. Join one beginner-friendly program

Choose a program that sells things in your topic and lets you join without an audience. Our comparison of seven beginner programs covers how each one works.

4. Choose one product, carefully

Your reputation is the real asset. Check the product, the sales page, the refund policy and the claims before you recommend anything. Our 10-question checklist walks through it.

5. Decide where your link lives

Most platforms let you put a link in your bio or description. A short bridge page between your post and the sales page can explain who the product is for. You don't need a full website to start; see affiliate marketing without a website.

6. Write your disclosure before your first post

Put it where people will see it before they click, in plain words. Our free disclosure generator gives you wording for each platform.

7. Post helpful content consistently

Answer real questions about the problem your product solves. Most posts won't mention the product at all, and that's fine: helpful posts are what earn attention. Choose a schedule you can keep for months rather than a burst you'll abandon in two weeks.

8. Read your numbers and adjust

Track views, link clicks and sales in your program's dashboard. If people watch but don't click, work on the reason to click. If they click but don't buy, look again at the product or the match with your audience. Our commission calculator helps you read click-to-sale ratios.

Skills you'll build along the way

  • Researching products and spotting hype
  • Writing short scripts and hooks
  • Making simple videos or graphics
  • Reading basic numbers: views, clicks, conversion
  • Following disclosure and platform rules

These are useful even if the commissions stay small.

How long does it take?

There's no honest fixed answer. Many people who try affiliate marketing earn little or nothing, especially at first, and results depend on your topic, products, competition, consistency and luck. Expect the first months to be mostly learning. Anyone promising you a first sale in days is selling something. For a fuller picture, see is affiliate marketing worth it?

Mistakes that slow beginners down

  • Joining many programs and promoting many products at once
  • Posting links with no helpful content around them
  • Repeating a vendor's income or health claims
  • Skipping disclosure, or hiding it where nobody sees it
  • Buying followers, courses or tools before testing the basics for free (see starting with no money)

Questions beginners ask

Can I become an affiliate marketer with no experience?

Yes. Most affiliate marketers started with none. The skills come from doing it: choosing products, making content and reading your numbers.

Is affiliate marketing a real job?

For some people it becomes a business; for most it starts as a side project. Treat it as something you're learning, not a guaranteed income.

Do I have to show my face?

No. Faceless formats such as voiceover with stock footage, screen recordings or text-on-screen videos work on most platforms, as long as you follow their rules and disclose.

How much do affiliate marketers make?

It varies enormously, and many earn nothing. We don't publish income figures or examples, because they'd tell you nothing reliable about your own results.
