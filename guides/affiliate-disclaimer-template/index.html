Affiliate Disclaimer Template: 6 Copy-Paste Versions

An affiliate disclaimer is a short statement, usually on its own page, explaining that you earn commissions from some links. Here are six templates you can copy, where to put them, and the one thing a disclaimer page can't do for you.

What is an affiliate disclaimer?

An affiliate disclaimer is a statement that tells visitors some of your links are affiliate links and that you may earn a commission if they buy. Most people put it on a dedicated page (often called "Affiliate Disclaimer" or "Disclosure Policy") and link to it from their site's footer or their link-in-bio page.

It's closely related to an affiliate disclosure, and people mix the two words up. The difference that matters:

Affiliate disclaimerAffiliate disclosure
Where it goesOne page on your site or link-in-bio pageIn each post, video, caption or email that has an affiliate link
What it doesExplains your affiliate relationships in fullTells people about the relationship at the moment they see the recommendation
Is it enough on its own?NoIt's the part the FTC focuses on

The FTC's guidance for creators says not to put your disclosure only on an "about me" or profile page, and that a disclosure should be hard to miss and placed with the endorsement itself. So a disclaimer page is a useful extra, not a replacement. For per-post wording, see our affiliate disclosure examples.

How to use these templates

  • Fill in everything in [square brackets].
  • Delete any sentence that isn't true for you. A template that says "I only recommend products I've used" is a false statement if you haven't used them.
  • Only keep "at no extra cost to you" if that's true for every program you're in.
  • Write it in plain words. Visitors should understand it without a dictionary.

Template 1: Short affiliate disclaimer

For a site footer, the top of a review post, or a link-in-bio page with only a few links.

Short versionSome links on this site are affiliate links. If you buy through them, I may earn a commission[, at no extra cost to you]. I always say when a link is an affiliate link.

Template 2: Full affiliate disclaimer page

For a dedicated page on your website. Keep the headings so people can scan it.

Affiliate disclaimer pageAffiliate Disclaimer

Last updated: [date]

[Site or brand name] is run by [your name or business name]. Some links on this site are affiliate links. That means if you click one and buy something, I may earn a commission from the seller.

Which programs I'm part of: [for example, Digistore24, Amazon Associates, a specific company's affiliate program].

How I choose what to recommend: [say what is actually true, for example: I read the full sales page, the refund policy and the affiliate terms before I link to anything. I say clearly when I haven't used a product myself.]

Prices: [Buying through my link does not change the price you pay. / Keep this only if it's true.]

In posts and videos: I also disclose affiliate links where they appear, not only on this page.

Questions: contact me at [email address].The "how I choose" part is optional, but if you include it, it has to describe what you really do.

Template 3: Link-in-bio page version

For a short-form video creator whose only "site" is a link-in-bio page. Put it right under the buttons, not at the bottom.

Under your buttonsSome of these are affiliate links: I earn a commission if you buy through them. [I've said in each video what I did and didn't check.]

Template 4: Amazon Associates statement

Amazon requires its own exact sentence for members of its program. Add it to your disclaimer page and wherever Amazon's current rules ask for it.

Amazon AssociatesAs an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.Only use this if you're in Amazon Associates. Check Amazon's current Operating Agreement for where it must appear.

Template 5: Email footer version

For a newsletter that sometimes includes affiliate links. It sits in the footer, but if a specific email recommends something, say so in that email too.

Email footerSome links in my emails are affiliate links. If you buy through one, I may earn a commission. I'll always say so next to the link.

Template 6: Earnings disclaimer (only if you mention money)

If your content ever mentions income, commissions or results, add an earnings disclaimer. It doesn't make an exaggerated claim acceptable: don't share figures you can't back up or that aren't what most people get.

Earnings disclaimerEarnings disclaimer: Nothing on this site is a promise that you'll earn money. [Any figures I share are examples to explain how commissions work, not predictions.] Many people who try affiliate marketing earn little or nothing. Your results depend on many things outside my control.

Where to put your affiliate disclaimer

  • Website: its own page, linked in the footer of every page.
  • Review or "best of" posts: a short version (Template 1) near the top, before the first affiliate link, with a link to the full page.
  • Link-in-bio page: right under your buttons (Template 3).
  • Email: in the footer (Template 5), plus a disclosure next to any link you recommend.
  • Videos and captions: a disclaimer page doesn't reach people here. Use a disclosure in the video and caption itself. Our free affiliate disclosure generator gives you wording for each platform.

What a disclaimer can't do

  • It doesn't replace the disclosure in each post or video.
  • It doesn't make a false or unproven claim acceptable. If you say a product works, the claim still has to be true.
  • It doesn't let you describe experiences with a product you haven't had.
  • It doesn't override an affiliate program's own rules. Read each program's terms.

Questions beginners ask

Is an affiliate disclaimer legally required?

In the US, the FTC's focus is a clear disclosure wherever you recommend something, not a separate page. Some affiliate programs do require specific wording, like Amazon's sentence above, and rules differ by country. A disclaimer page is a good habit; per-post disclosure is the part you can't skip. This isn't legal advice, so check the rules where you live.

Do I need an affiliate disclaimer if I don't have a website?

You don't need a website for it. If you use a link-in-bio page, put a short version under your buttons. In each video or caption, use a disclosure.

Can I copy an affiliate disclaimer from another site?

Don't copy someone else's wording word for word; it may say things that aren't true for you. Use these templates as a starting point and edit them so every sentence describes what you actually do.

Is "at no extra cost to you" always true?

Usually, but not always, so check. If a program's price through your link could differ from the price elsewhere, leave that phrase out.

Does an affiliate disclaimer protect me from liability?

Not in the way people hope. A disclaimer can explain your relationships, but it won't protect a misleading post. The safest approach is honest content plus a clear disclosure each time.
