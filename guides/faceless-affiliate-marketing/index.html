Faceless Affiliate Marketing for Beginners: How to Do It Without Showing Your Face

Yes, you can do affiliate marketing without ever showing your face. Here's how faceless affiliate marketing works, the video formats beginners use, content ideas, and the rules that still apply when nobody sees you.

Can you do affiliate marketing without showing your face?

Yes. Affiliate marketing only needs three things: something useful to say, a place to say it, and a link with an honest disclosure. None of that requires a face on camera. Plenty of creators teach, review and explain things using screen recordings, text and a voiceover. Every short video on our own channels is made that way, with an AI voice that we label.

What being faceless doesn't change: you still have to be useful, you still have to disclose your affiliate links, and results are never guaranteed. Faceless removes the camera, not the work.

How faceless affiliate marketing works

  1. Pick one offer you can recommend honestly. Our guide on how to find affiliate products to promote has a 10-question checklist for this.
  2. Set up one faceless account on a short-form platform such as TikTok or Instagram. Pick the one you already know.
  3. Make short videos that teach one thing, using one of the formats below.
  4. Put your link in your profile, either straight to the offer or through a short bridge page that explains it.
  5. Disclose every time, in the video and in the caption.
  6. Look at your numbers after a few weeks and make more of what people actually watched.

The three ingredients of a faceless video

1. Visuals

Your own screen recordings, simple slides, or stock footage. If you use stock footage, choose a source whose license allows commercial use and read the terms. Videos with affiliate links usually count as commercial.

2. Text

Short on-screen lines with the key points, plus captions. Many people watch with the sound off, so the video should still make sense on mute.

3. Audio

Your own voice, or a text-to-speech (AI) voice. If you use an AI voice tool, check its license first: some free plans don't allow commercial use or require attribution. Only use music you have the right to use on the platform you post to.

A simple structure for every video

Most short faceless videos follow the same three parts:

  • Hook (first few seconds): a specific, honest reason to keep watching. "Three things to check before you buy any online course."
  • Value (the middle): teach one useful idea. One tip, one checklist, one explanation.
  • Soft call to action (the end): an invitation, not pressure, with the disclosure built in.
Example (about 30 seconds): "Before you buy any online course, check these three things. One: can you see the full price and what you get before checkout? Two: is there a clear refund policy? Three: are there any guaranteed-income promises? If so, skip it. I've put a free checklist in my profile. Some links there are affiliate links, so I may earn a commission."

Avoid hooks that invent results or experiences, like claiming you earned a certain amount. If it didn't happen, don't say it.

Faceless content ideas for affiliate marketing

Ideas that work without a face, and without making claims you can't back up:

  • "3 things to check before you buy [type of product]"
  • A screen-recorded walkthrough of setting up a tool you actually use
  • "Beginner mistakes with [topic]" as a text-over-b-roll list
  • A side-by-side comparison of two options, with honest pros and cons
  • "What I'd want to know before buying [product]", answered from the sales page and the refund policy
  • Myth vs fact in your niche
  • A one-minute summary of a free resource, pointing people to the full version

Best niches for faceless affiliate marketing

Topics that tend to suit beginners have steady demand and plenty of beginner-friendly products: personal organization and productivity, hobbies and skills, software tools, and everyday personal finance basics such as budgeting. These also lend themselves to screen recordings and lists.

Take extra care with health, weight loss, relationship advice and anything about making money online, where exaggerated claims are common and regulators pay attention. Avoid, for now, anything that needs professional qualifications to advise on, such as medical, legal or investment products.

Setting up a faceless TikTok or Instagram account

  • Username: clean, easy to remember, relevant to your topic. Avoid names that suggest guaranteed results.
  • Profile picture: a simple logo or icon. No face needed.
  • Bio: one line about who you help and what you share, and say up front that you use affiliate links, e.g. "Beginner tips on [topic]. I earn commissions from some links."
  • Time limit: don't spend more than an hour on setup. A perfect profile won't make up for having nothing to post.

What doesn't change when you're faceless

You still have to disclose

FTC disclosure rules apply to faceless posts exactly as they do to anyone else. Say it in the video, show it on screen, and put it at the start of the caption. See our affiliate disclosure examples for wording you can copy.

Label AI where the platform asks

If you use AI-generated visuals or an AI voice, TikTok and Instagram both have ways to label AI content and ask creators to use them in certain cases. Check each platform's current rules and label your content when they apply.

Only say what's true

Don't talk about experiences with a product you haven't used, and don't invent testimonials or results. Being faceless can make it tempting to sound more experienced than you are. Resist it. Trust is the only thing a faceless account has to build on.

Questions beginners ask

Can you earn money with faceless affiliate marketing?

Some people do, but there is no guarantee, and many people who try affiliate marketing earn nothing. Organic reach is unpredictable, and the first weeks are mostly practice. Treat it as a skill you build, not a quick result.

Can I use AI to make faceless videos?

Yes, many creators use AI voices or AI video tools. Check that the tool's license allows commercial use, label AI content where the platform asks, and review every video before posting so it doesn't say or show anything untrue.

Do I need a website for faceless affiliate marketing?

No. Many beginners use a profile link, either straight to the offer or to a simple link-in-bio or bridge page. You still need to follow each platform's rules and the affiliate program's terms.

How often should I post?

Daily posting for the first month is a common suggestion because every video is practice, but it's demanding. A schedule you can keep is better than one you abandon after a week.
