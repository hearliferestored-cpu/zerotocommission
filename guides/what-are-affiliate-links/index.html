What Are Affiliate Links? How They Work, With Examples

An affiliate link is an ordinary web address with a tag added that tells the seller who sent the buyer. Here's what's inside one, how the tracking works, where you can post it, and the rules that come with it.

What is an affiliate link?

An affiliate link is a URL that points to a product page and includes your unique ID. When someone clicks it and buys, the seller's tracking system matches the sale to your ID and pays you a commission under the program's terms. To the visitor it opens the same product page as a normal link.

You get your links from the program's dashboard after you join. You don't build them yourself.

What's inside an affiliate link

Formats differ by program, but most links have the same parts. Here's an illustrative example (not a real link):

Examplehttps://example-shop.com/product/blender?ref=yourname123&campaign=shorts
  • The destination: the page the visitor lands on (example-shop.com/product/blender).
  • Your affiliate ID: the tag that identifies you (ref=yourname123). This is the part that gets you credited.
  • Optional tracking labels: many programs let you add a label (campaign=shorts) so you can see which post or platform a sale came from.

Some programs give you a redirect link on their own domain instead. It works the same way: it records the click, then forwards the visitor to the product.

How affiliate tracking works

  1. A visitor clicks your link.
  2. The seller's system records the click and usually stores a small cookie or similar identifier in the visitor's browser.
  3. If the visitor buys within the program's tracking window, the sale is credited to you.
  4. After any refund period, the commission becomes payable under the program's payout terms.

The tracking window, sometimes called cookie duration, is set by each program and can range from a day to several months. Many programs credit the last affiliate link the buyer clicked. Tracking isn't perfect: browser privacy settings, switching devices or clearing cookies can mean a sale isn't credited.

Do affiliate links cost the buyer more?

Usually not. The commission normally comes out of the seller's margin, so the buyer pays the same price. Check this for each program before you say it, though, because it's a claim you're making to your audience.

Where you can put affiliate links

That depends on the platform and the program. Links in TikTok and Instagram captions and in YouTube Shorts descriptions aren't clickable, for example, and Amazon doesn't allow its links in emails. Our free link rules checker gives you the answer for each platform, with sources.

The rules that come with affiliate links

  • Disclose every time. In the US, the FTC expects a clear statement that you earn from the link, placed where people see it before they click. See disclosure examples.
  • Mark links on your website. Google asks sites to add rel="sponsored" (or rel="nofollow") to affiliate links.
  • Don't disguise the destination. Pinterest doesn't allow certain redirects and URL shorteners, and Amazon requires that its links don't hide that they go to Amazon.
  • Follow the program's terms, including where you're allowed to promote and which claims you can make.

Affiliate link vs. referral link vs. promo code

TypeHow it's trackedTypical use
Affiliate linkA tagged URLContent creators recommending products for a commission
Referral linkA tagged URL, often for an app or serviceCustomers inviting friends, often for credit or a bonus
Promo codeA code typed at checkoutPlaces where links don't work, such as podcasts or video captions

All three need disclosure when you benefit from them.

Questions beginners ask

How do I get an affiliate link?

Join an affiliate program, then generate your link for a specific product in its dashboard. See our comparison of affiliate programs for beginners.

Are affiliate links safe to click?

Links from legitimate programs simply open the seller's page. As with any link, be careful with shortened links from accounts you don't know.

Can I shorten my affiliate links?

Some programs and platforms allow it and some don't. Pinterest restricts certain shorteners, and Amazon requires that links don't hide that they go to Amazon. Check both sets of rules.

Do affiliate links expire?

The link itself usually keeps working while the product and your account exist. The tracking window after each click is what's limited, and it's set by the program.
